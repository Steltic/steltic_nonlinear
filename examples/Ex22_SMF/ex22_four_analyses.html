One hospital moment frame, four analyses: what each package proves about Ex22

Ex22_SMF is the Steltic gold example of a 6-storey Risk Category IV hospital: perimeter special moment frames on all four faces, RBS connections, fixed SMF column bases, SDS = 1.0 g, R = 8, Ie = 1.5, drift-governed at the 1.0% RC IV limit. Unlike wind-governed Ex18, this building is designed to yield. The same design of record has been run through the Steltic member design (AISC 360/341 LRFD), the Pushover Analyst (ASCE 41-23 NSP with AISC 342-22 component parameters read from the converted standards), the Non Linear Dynamic Bot (ASCE 7-22 Chapter 16, 11 MCER pairs) and the DDM system-capacity analysis (GMNIA). This sheet puts the four answers side by side.

The short version. All four say the hospital stands, and the three nonlinear methods agree on how: it yields where an SMF is meant to — in the RBS beam hinges of storeys 1–4 — keeps every column elastic, and drifts about 1½% at MCER, roughly 1.6 × the 0.91% design drift. The pushover and the eleven-record suite tell the same story (the coefficient-method target displacement lands 19% above the record mean in Y and 34% above it in X — on the conservative side, as the NSP usually is for a first-mode frame — with storey-drift profiles of the same shape) and the Chapter 16 suite passes the Risk Category IV rules — mean drift 1.46% against 2.00%, all eleven responses acceptable — but three individual records exceed 2% and the strongest reaches 2.13%, so the margin lives in the averaging, not in the members. The DDM, as on Ex18, points elsewhere: the thinnest margin under factored load is the gravity system.

Where they agree, where they cannot, and the one result to read twice

SHEET 4

The nonlinear seismic methods agree — and confirm the design intent

The pushover finds a clean beam-hinge mechanism: at the BSE-2N target displacement every SMF beam end in storeys 1–4 has yielded (24 of 40 hinges per level in X, 16 in Y), the base column hinges stay below θy, and the worst RBS hinge sits at 1.40 × IO but only 0.28 × LS and 0.21 × CP. The RC IV BPON (ASCE 41-23 Table 2-5: IO at BSE-1N, LS at BSE-2N) passes in both directions with D/C 0.44 / 0.66 and 0.27 / 0.28. The dynamic suite reproduces the picture: the same storeys drift most, the mean roof displacement in Y (10.9 in) sits 19% below δt (13.0 in), the mean beam plastic rotation of the worst group is 0.0080 rad against a CP of 0.0560 rad, and the SMF columns remain force-controlled at D/C 0.40.

The number that matters: storey drift against the Risk Category IV limit

Because Ex22 is Risk Category IV, Chapter 16 uses the RC IV row of Table 12.12-1: the permissible mean storey drift is 2 × 0.010 = 2.00%, half of what an ordinary building gets, and no unacceptable response is allowed among the eleven records (16.4.1.1 grants the one-record exception only to RC I and II). The suite mean is 1.46% at storeys 2–3 in Y, 0.54 points under the limit; individually, 3 of the eleven records exceed 2% (up to 2.13%), all well inside the 3% (150% of the limit) that would make a single response unacceptable. The pushover reads the same building at 1.83% at δt BSE-2N — the coefficient method lands near the upper records rather than the mean, which is its usual bias for a first-mode-dominated frame at Te ≈ 1.1 s. A hospital designed to the letter of the 1.0% RC IV drift limit under the R-reduced design earthquake therefore keeps about a quarter of its Chapter 16 drift margin at MCER; the linear package, whose Cdδe/Ie of 0.91% would scale to 1.36% at MCER, happens to land near the suite mean (1.46%) — but it cannot see the pushover's 1.83% at δt or the 2.13% of the strongest record, and it has no way of knowing that the hinges have formed.

Non-converged records and what they mean. The first Chapter 16 attempt (Newmark average acceleration, dt = 0.02 s) diverged numerically in two records — storey displacements alternating in sign between floors, the signature of a high-frequency instability of stiff hinge springs, not of a collapse. The bot's tool now integrates with HHT-α (α = 0.9), halves the time step adaptively and re-runs a non-converged record at half the step; at dt = 0.01 s ten of the eleven records converged without a single failed step. The eleventh (Northridge Canyon Country) still stopped at the time-step floor at dt = 0.01 and 0.005 s, each time with a spuriously growing drift, and converged cleanly at dt = 0.0025 s with a peak drift of 1.58% — in line with its neighbours. It is carried as acceptable on that basis and the retry is recorded in the package. As a check on the step size itself, a converged record (Kocaeli Duzce) was re-run at dt = 0.0025 s: peak drifts 1.31% / 2.00% against 1.37% / 2.02% at dt = 0.01 s, roof 9.1 / 13.2 in against 8.8 / 14.1 in — the suite results are not step-size artefacts. Telling numerical non-convergence from a genuine dynamic instability is exactly the judgement the Non Linear Dynamic Bot is asked to make and disclose; the rule it applied here is "a record that converges at a finer step with a physically consistent response is not an unacceptable response".

What the DDM adds — and what it cannot say here

The GMNIA sweeps pass the elastic transfer gate at 1.5% and put λu = 1.84 on the governing gravity combination (1.2D+1.6L+0.5Lr), failing by inelastic instability of fifteen W14X193 gravity columns; with the verified RC IV system factor φs = 0.70 (βT = 3.5, SSRC 2013 Table 10) the check is 1.29 ≥ 1.0. That is a comfortable pass, and it confirms Steltic's own reading that the gravity system, not the seismic system, carries the smallest margin — the D/C 0.90 floor beams and the D/C 0.62 W14X193 columns. For the lateral combinations the DDM is less useful on this building: because λ multiplies gravity as well as the lateral pattern, the 12 lateral cases that carry full gravity (1.2D + 0.5L with seismic or wind) reach a geometric (P-Δ) peak at λ = 2.17–3.46 with no member yielded, the 0.9D seismic cases yield their beams at λ ≈ 2.8–3.4 and peak at λu 4.3–4.3, and the seismic cases carry no φs at all (R > 3 is outside the calibrations). Lateral system capacity is the pushover's question, not the DDM's.

Where the four cannot be reconciled — by design

  • Strength. Steltic's D/C 0.14–0.31 on the SMF members is not a strength surplus of 3–7 ×; it is the price of the RC IV drift limit. The pushover's Ω 5.5 / 4.3 is the same fact measured at the system level. The DDM's λu for lateral cases measures something else (P-Δ of scaled gravity) and should not be quoted as overstrength.
  • Deformation. The design drift (0.91%), the NSP drift (1.83%) and the Chapter 16 mean (1.46%) are three different quantities — design earthquake with Cd/Ie, MCER coefficient method, MCER record mean — and only the last has a limit that is nearly binding.
  • Post-peak behaviour. The pushover's descending branch is a lower bound (μT 2.71 / 2.51 lower bound; the solver stopped at V/Vmax 0.83 / 0.97). The NLRHA sees no strength loss because no hinge reaches its capping rotation a; the DDM sees a plateau for gravity and none for lateral.
