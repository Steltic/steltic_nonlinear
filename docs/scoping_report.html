What a pushover analysis is

SHEET 1 · one page

A pushover — the nonlinear static procedure (NSP) in US standards — is a displacement-controlled static analysis of a building model whose members are allowed to yield. Gravity is applied first and held. A fixed pattern of lateral forces, usually proportional to the first mode shape times the floor masses, is then scaled up monotonically while the roof (the control node) is pushed to ever larger displacement. At each step the model redistributes force as hinges form, so the result is not a single load case but a whole history: base shear plotted against roof displacement, the capacity curve.

The curve is elastic and straight until the first hinges yield, softens as a mechanism develops, peaks at the system's real strength Vmax, and then falls as hinges reach their capping rotation and P-Δ takes over. Three things are read from it. Strength: Vmax compared with the design base shear gives the true overstrength Ω. Stiffness: a bilinear idealisation of the curve gives an effective stiffness Ke and period Te. Deformation capacity: how far the structure can go before component rotations exceed their limits or the curve loses 20% of its peak.

What makes the analysis nonlinear is the component model. In steel frames each beam and column end carries a concentrated plastic hinge with a backbone curve defined by an expected yield strength (Z·RyFy), a small hardening slope, a plastic rotation a at which strength caps, a residual strength ratio c, and a rotation b at which the hinge stops carrying gravity. Acceptance is checked on the same axis: plastic-rotation limits for Immediate Occupancy, Life Safety and Collapse Prevention. Braces get axial backbones with buckling; links, plates and BRB cores get their own. Column hinges shrink with axial load, and heavily loaded columns become force-controlled — they may not hinge at all.

Demand is put on the curve by the target displacement: the roof displacement an equivalent single-degree-of-freedom oscillator with period Te would reach under the design spectrum, δt = C0C1C2·Sa·(Te²/4π²)·g. C0 converts SDOF spectral displacement to roof displacement, C1 corrects elastic to inelastic displacement for short-period systems, C2 covers pinched and degrading hysteresis. The structure is pushed to at least 150% of δt, and every hinge rotation, story drift, column axial and the mechanism itself are read at δt.

Limits. One load pattern cannot represent higher modes, so the NSP is trusted only for regular, first-mode-dominated structures and must be supplemented by a linear dynamic check where higher modes matter. It knows nothing of duration or cyclic deterioration, and the target displacement is an estimate with real scatter. It is, however, the cheapest analysis that reveals a mechanism, and that is why it sits between elastic design and full response-history analysis.

How pushover analysis is used in US steel design

SHEET 2 · one page

The method entered US practice through the retrofit world. ATC-40 (1996) framed it as the capacity-spectrum method for concrete buildings; FEMA 273 (1997) and FEMA 356 (2000) generalised it as the coefficient method with the C0–C3 factors and the a-b-c hinge tables; FEMA 440 (2005) re-calibrated C1 and C2 against 180,000 nonlinear response histories, replaced C3 with a maximum-strength-ratio limit on dynamic instability, and stated plainly that nonlinear static procedures cannot reliably estimate higher-mode (MDOF) effects. ASCE/SEI 41 then codified the result — 41-06, 41-13, 41-17 and now ASCE/SEI 41-23, where the NSP is one of four analysis procedures (LSP, LDP, NSP, NDP) inside a Tier 3 systematic evaluation.

Existing buildings. This is the NSP's home. ASCE 41 permits it when the strength ratio μstrength stays below μmax (otherwise dynamic instability governs and an NDP is required) and requires a linear dynamic supplement where higher modes are significant. Component acceptance is deformation-controlled (m-factors in linear procedures, plastic-rotation limits in nonlinear ones) or force-controlled (γχ(QUF−QG)+QG ≤ QCL). The steel-specific content — expected and lower-bound material factors, beam/column/brace/link modelling parameters and IO/LS/CP criteria — moved out of ASCE 41's Chapter 9 into ANSI/AISC 342-22, Seismic Provisions for Evaluation and Retrofit of Existing Structural Steel Buildings, which ASCE 41-23 references the way ASCE 7 references AISC 341. AISC 342 is a free download; it carries the column expressions (rotation capacity falling with PG/Pye and slenderness) developed in the NIST/ATC work that fed ASCE 41-17.

New buildings. ASCE 7-22 contains no pushover procedure. Its Chapter 16 is nonlinear response history analysis only — at least eleven ground motions at MCER, with independent structural design review. A pushover reaches a new steel building through three doors: (1) §1.3.1.3 performance-based alternatives, where ASCE 41's BPON objective (Life Safety at BSE-1N, Collapse Prevention at BSE-2N) is the usual reference procedure and the AHJ and peer reviewer decide; (2) the tall-building and PBSD guidelines (PEER TBI, LATBSDC), where a pushover is used to understand the mechanism before the required NLRHA; and (3) FEMA P-695, the system-qualification methodology behind new rows of ASCE 7 Table 12.2-1 — its overstrength Ω = Vmax/V and period-based ductility μT = δu/δy,eff (δu at 20% strength loss) are read from archetype pushovers, and μT ≥ 3 sets the spectral-shape credit. FEMA P-58 accepts a simplified pushover-based analysis for loss estimation.

Bridges. The AASHTO Guide Specifications for LRFD Seismic Bridge Design make the pushover mandatory in the highest seismic design category: each pier's displacement capacity ΔC from a pushover must exceed the displacement demand ΔD from elastic analysis.

Where it lands for AISC steel systems. AISC 341 designs the mechanism by rule — strong-column/weak-beam ratios, capacity-limited brace connections, Ω0QE for columns and collectors — without ever drawing the curve. A pushover is how an engineer verifies that the intended mechanism actually forms, how large the real overstrength is (drift-governed frames routinely exceed Ω0), and what the inelastic drift at MCER is. Modelling follows NIST GCR 17-917-46 (Part I general; Part IIa steel moment frames) — concentrated hinges with the Lignos-Krawinkler deterioration model, panel zones, composite slab effects — usually in OpenSees, PERFORM-3D, SAP2000/ETABS.

The practical consequence for Steltic. The linear package proves strength (D/C ≤ 1), drift (Cdδe/Ie) and prescriptive detailing. It cannot show the mechanism, the real Ω, the inelastic drift at MCER, or deformation-based component acceptance. Those four things are precisely what a pushover supplement adds — provided the hinge parameters come from AISC 342-22 and ASCE 41-23, not from memory.

Agent scope — from Steltic's download package to a pushover supplement

SHEET 3

Steltic's pipeline (pipeline.design_and_report) writes a job folder that bundle.make_zip ships as the download. Reading the repo, the package already contains everything a pushover needs except the nonlinear component data: the exact elastic OpenSees model is replayed command-by-command in model_opensees.py (nodes, fixes, masses, PDelta transforms, elasticBeamColumn members, rigid diaphragms); design/member_schedule.csv maps every element tag to its kind and AISC section; cfg.py carries SDS, SD1, R, Cd, Ω0, Ie, the declared system and loads; report.html carries W, the ELF base shear and the design period. The agent's contract is therefore a converter, not a re-modeller.

Pipeline stages

  1. Inspect — parse the package; refuse to run if SDS, SD1, W or the system is missing or weakly sourced.
  2. Gravity pre-run — replay the elastic model, apply QG = 1.1(D + 0.25L) per level, read each column's PG.
  3. Convert — each column/beam becomes an elastic interior element (I × 11/10, original P-Δ transform kept) plus two zero-length strong-axis hinges (K0 = 10·6EI/L, IMKPeakOriented, monotonic). Steltic end releases are honoured; force-controlled columns get no hinge; braces stay elastic and are flagged (prototype).
  4. Push — first translational mode × mass in X, then Y; displacement control at the roof master; step-halving and algorithm fallback; stop at max drift, 20% strength loss, or solver failure.
  5. Evaluate — bilinear idealisation iterated with δt; C0 from Γ1φroof, C1, C2, Cm; μmax applicability; Ω, μT; plastic rotation of every hinge at δt vs IO/LS/CP; mechanism census; story drifts; column P.
  6. Report — HTML + JSON supplement. A red banner stays until hinge_params.json is verified: true.

Acceptance gates (analogue of Steltic's completion gate)

  • Design basis complete and sourced; hinge parameters verified against retrieved text.
  • Curve reaches ≥ 1.5 δt at BSE-2N; μstrength ≤ μmax; higher-mode check stated.
  • Mechanism reconciled with the package's SCWB / capacity-design block.
  • LS at BSE-1N and CP at BSE-2N reported per hinge group; force-controlled columns listed.
  • Supplement says what it does not change: the AISC 360/341 member and connection checks.

Reading. The curve tells the truth the ELF package cannot: this archetype is so drift-governed that its columns and beams stay near yield at MCER, and Ω0QE is nowhere near an upper bound for its capacity-designed elements. Only beam hinges yield at δt; column hinges appear at the base only — the strong-column mechanism AISC 341 intends.

Known gaps in the prototype (roadmap)

  • Frames only. SCBF/OCBF/EBF/BRBF/SPSW need brace (corotational truss + Steel02 + camber), shear-link and BRB-core models; blocks are stubbed in hinge_params.json.
  • Rigid panel zones, bare centreline, no composite slab, no fracture, fixed bases — the same idealisations as the linear model; NIST GCR 17-917-46v2 guidance to be added for SMF.
  • Gravity spread equally over each level's column nodes (footprint from node extents); irregular plans should take tributary loads from model_static.py.
  • Descending branch: the tool first tests whether hinges reached rotation b (loss of gravity capacity — a valid FEMA P-695 end point, as in the X push), then escalates through a fine-step and an arc-length rung; if both fail (the Y push) it flags lower_bound and the bot asks the user before rung 3, --post-cap-ratio, which is a disclosed modelling change. The descending branch affects δu, μT and α2 only — not δt or acceptance below capping.
  • Higher-mode LDP supplement and the force-controlled Eq. 7-38 check are listed as open items rather than computed.

The Pushover Analyst as a fourth Grok Bot

SHEET 4

steltic_grokbot runs three bots with a strict division of labour: Query file manager converts the user's licensed PDFs with Docling and answers JSON retrieval plans with verbatim excerpts (no vector database, found: false is an honest answer); HR Steel App and CFS Steel App design buildings by following Steltic's contract/AGENT_START.md and compute only from returned text. The Pushover Analyst joins as a fourth bot on the same rules. It loads the existing retrieval skill unchanged plus one new skill, and it drives one new tool.

HR Steel AppDesigns to AISC 360/341/358; produces the download package. Unchanged.
CFS Steel AppCold-formed steel. Unchanged (a CFS pushover is out of scope here).
Pushover AnalystTakes the HR package, retrieves ASCE 41-23 / AISC 342-22 parameters, runs python -m pushover, judges, delivers the supplement.

The bot's job cycle

  1. python -m pushover inspect — confirm basis and system.
  2. Send wave-1 JSON plan to Query file manager (NSP clauses, C-coefficients, Cm/C0 tables, QG, μmax; AISC 342 beam/column/brace tables; Ry; ASCE 7 Table 12.2-1).
  3. Fill hinge_params.json from excerpts; set verified only when every needed block is cited.
  4. python -m pushover run; judge NSP applicability, mechanism vs SCWB, acceptance, Ω/μT, drift.
  5. Wave-2 digit-by-digit verification; deliver pushover/ plus narrative and open items.

Design decision worth stating. The tool deliberately ships with wrong-by-default placeholders and a banner rather than "good enough" numbers. In this harness the value of the bot is that no φ, γ, C-coefficient or rotation limit reaches a report without a printed-page citation; a convincing default would defeat that.
