One braced frame, four analyses: what each package proves about Ex18

Ex18_R3 is the Steltic gold example of an 8-storey, R = 3 conventional steel building — perimeter X-braced bays, everything pinned, wind-governed, SDC B. The same design of record has now been run through four independent packages: the Steltic member design (AISC 360 LRFD), the Pushover Analyst (ASCE 41 NSP), the Non Linear Dynamic Bot (ASCE 7-22 Chapter 16 NLRHA) and the DDM system-capacity analysis (GMNIA). This sheet puts their answers side by side and says where they agree, where they cannot, and what each one adds that the others cannot see.

The short version. All four say the building stands. They disagree about what is close to the edge. Steltic and DDM both point at the W14X109 gravity columns under factored gravity (D/C 0.96; φsλu 1.09). The two seismic analyses find a lateral system with five to six times the strength the R = 3 design ever asked of it, whose braces nevertheless buckle under MCER shaking — harmlessly, at drifts of about half a percent, because the tension diagonals and the pinned frame have somewhere to go. Nobody would learn that from the linear package.

How they complement each other — and where they do not

SHEET 4

Where they agree

The elastic backbone is the same building four times: periods within 2%, the same pinned load path, the same wind-driven brace sizes. Every nonlinear method — monotonic, dynamic and system-capacity — puts the first nonlinearity in the slender HSS diagonals, at roughly the same displacement (3–4 in of roof, 0.4–0.5% story drift), and none finds a story mechanism or column hinging under lateral load. The R = 3 design's implicit promise, that a non-detailed system stays essentially elastic at design level and survives MCER by strength rather than ductility, holds in all three nonlinear pictures.

Where they diverge, and why that is useful

  1. Brace effective length. Steltic rates the X-braces on half the diagonal (the crossing point braces the compression diagonal); pushover, NLRHA and DDM all use K = 1 on the full diagonal because the model has no crossing node. Steltic's brace D/C of 0.45 is therefore not comparable with the nonlinear buckling loads — the design assumption is the more favourable one, and the nonlinear models are the conservative check on it. Adding the crossing node (or a mid-length restraint) is the single modelling change that would reconcile all four.
  2. "Strength" means different things. The pushover's Vmax (1,694 kip) credits the tension diagonals after the compression ones have buckled; DDM's λu stops at the first limit point (1.95 × the seismic combination) and does not credit post-buckling redistribution. Both are right for their purpose: the NSP wants the whole curve to locate δt; a design-by-advanced-analysis check wants the first loss of stability.
  3. Gravity is invisible to the seismic tools. The Steltic package (D/C 0.96) and DDM (φsλu = 1.09, inelastic column instability at λ = 1.28) both flag the W14X109 gravity columns under 1.2D + 1.6L + 0.5Lr. Pushover and NLRHA never load that combination — they carry 1.1(D + 0.25L) or 1.0D + 0.5L and push sideways. A reader who only had the two seismic supplements would think Ex18's margin lives in its braces; it lives in its interior columns.
  4. NSP versus NLRHA. The NSP target displacements (3.0 / 3.4 in) and the suite means (3.1 / 3.1 in) agree to within 10%, and both put the braces just past buckling with CP demand-to-capacity of 0.2–0.3. That agreement is expected for a regular, first-mode-dominated building at a strength ratio near 2, and it is the case in which ASCE 41 lets the NSP stand alone. What the dynamic suite adds is the distribution: individual records reach 0.72% story drift and 5.6 in of roof displacement — nearly twice the NSP target — with no record unacceptable and a mean drift a tenth of the Chapter 16 limit. It also confirms that the pushover’s captured descending branch (X) is academic here: no record comes near the tension-yield plateau, let alone the post-peak drop.
  5. Provisional numbers. The two Grok-Bot supplements carry unverified ASCE 41 / AISC 342 backbones and the DDM carries provisional φs; only Steltic's checks are fully cited. The harness rule — nothing reaches a stamped report without a retrieved excerpt — is what turns these from four opinions into one auditable file set.
What the set proves together. Strength for every code combination (Steltic) · a benign mechanism and generous overstrength (Pushover) · acceptable MCER response with margin against Chapter 16 limits (NLRHA) · a system-level safety margin that includes imperfections and residual stresses (DDM). The one open engineering question they jointly raise is the gravity columns: a D/C of 0.96 and a φsλu of 1.09 are the same warning in two languages, and neither seismic supplement can answer it.

What to do with Ex18 next

  • Add the X-brace crossing node (or declare the half-length assumption) in the Steltic cfg so all four models share one brace capacity.
  • Have the Pushover Analyst and Non Linear Dynamic Bot retrieve AISC 342-22 brace parameters and Ry for A500 through Query file manager; rerun both (they read the same hinge_params.json).
  • Run the Chapter 16 no-live-load case (16.3.2) — not required for Ex18 (Σ0.5L = 15% of ΣD and L0 = 65 psf, so the 16.3.2 exception applies) — and a hazard-consistent record set for the actual site (16.2.2).
  • Treat the W14X109 gravity column group as the item for the reviewer: either accept the 4% margin or step one size.

The Non Linear Dynamic Bot and the file set

SHEET 5

The newest Grok Bot in the set, Non Linear Dynamic Bot, lives in the new repository steltic_nlrha and reuses the Pushover Analyst's hinge model — one model, three analyses. Its tool implements ASCE 7-22 Chapter 16 as read against your converted ASCE 7-22 (pdf pp. 248–251): MCER target by Method 1 (1.5 × design spectrum, 11.4.6), ≥ 11 pairs, period range [min(0.2 Tmin, T90%), 2 Tmax], RotD100 amplitude scaling with the 90% floor, ±10% orientation balance, 1.0D + 0.5L gravity with the live-load rule and exception, ≤ 2.5% damping, and the 16.4 acceptance (unacceptable-response count, mean drift with the height formula, force-controlled Eq. 16.4-1/-2, deformation-controlled CP and valid range).
